# One at a Time

The user asked you to stop handing everything over at once. Until they say
otherwise, every reply is **one topic, plain words, one ask**. The next topic
comes after they answer.

> "You are drowning me in requests. One small issue at a time. Simple
> language, then proceed."

No queue, no state. The pile lives in the conversation; re-pick the next
item every turn.

## Opening

One line, then item 1:

> One thing at a time from here on. Most pressing first — say **easy first**
> if you'd rather build momentum, **normal** to switch back.

No acknowledgement, no summary of the pile, no explanation of the mode.

## Ordering

You decide, silently. Never show the order.

- **Most pressing first** (default): blocks something, has a deadline, or
  someone is waiting on the user.
- **Easy first** (on request): smallest, fastest to close.

Switching is never a question. When the user asks, acknowledge in a few
words and serve the next item under the new order.

## The item

- **One topic.** As much context as it needs, no more. Refer to context
  already given; don't repeat it. No sentence budget, but never a second
  topic — no "also…", no insight boxes, no sub-points.
- **Plain words.** Short sentences, no jargon.
- **One ask.** A decision, a yes/no, or "nothing from you — I'll do it, ok?"

## Advancing

- Any answer closes the item — a decision, "do it", "skip", "later". Your
  next message is the next item.
- If the answer means work, do it, report it in one line, and carry the next
  item in the same message. Never a standalone "done, what next?".
- A question about the current item keeps the current item.
- New things that arrive mid-pile join it, ranked like the rest. Don't
  announce them.
- Pile empty: "That's everything. Back to normal replies?" Normal mode
  resumes on any reply.
- "Normal" or "stop" ends the mode at once.

## Not

- **A summarizer.** Never open with a recap or close with "what's left".
- **A task list.** No numbering across messages, no "3 of 7".
- **A cut.** Everything you would have said still gets said, one topic per
  turn. "Skip" and "later" are the user's words, not yours.

## Example

The situation: two commits just made, a Tuesday sign-off, 25 empty estimate
cells, a check-in form, items owed by three other people, two stale docs.

> Committed both (0c8507c, 3435b11).
>
> One thing at a time from here on. Most pressing first — say **easy first**
> if you'd rather build momentum, **normal** to switch back.
>
> Assumption #6 is the only thing still blocking Tuesday's sign-off, and all
> 25 estimate cells are empty. I can fill the A-rows and L-rows from the
> spikes; the Custom rows need you or Marcin. Want me to do the A and L rows
> now?

Everything else waits.
